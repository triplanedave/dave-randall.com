19 days. One theme: **make Intune automation intentional.**

Over this series, I walked through how teams can move from *calling APIs* to *operating platforms* with Microsoft Graph and Intune.

The key element wasn't API syntax. It was discipline:

- ✅ Understand Graph fundamentals and OData so queries are precise, fast, and readable ([Days 1–3](/graph/day-01/))
- ✅ Design auth and permissions with least privilege in mind, not just `.ReadWrite.All` ([Days 2 and 5](/graph/day-05/))
- ✅ Treat automation like production software: resilient retries, idempotency, and safe rollouts ([Days 4, 13, and 17](/graph/day-13/))
- ✅ Use RBAC, scopes, and approval patterns to reduce blast radius ([Day 14](/graph/day-14/))
- ✅ Turn raw device data into signals leaders can act on ([Days 11 and 18](/graph/day-18/))
- ✅ Close the loop with reporting, notifications, and governance ([Days 10, 15, and 16](/graph/day-16/))

Automation at scale isn't about more scripts. It's about **clear intent, controlled access, and trustworthy signals**.

If you build on Graph, manage Intune at scale, or design admin platforms, these patterns aren't optional anymore. They're table stakes.
